A workspace is the window of panes you work in. Launches land as tabs in one, and a workspace holds references to the running subshells rather than copies of them.

## How they fit together

A subshell is the session; a workspace is how you arrange sessions. The same subshell can sit in several workspaces at once, because a workspace holds a reference, not a copy. Closing a pane in a workspace removes that reference and leaves the session running; closing the subshell itself ends it everywhere (see [Session lifecycle](/guides/lifecycle)).

Launch your first subshell and you're in a workspace, named after it, with nothing to save. Removing its final tab retires the workspace, and the session keeps running unfiled. Closing the subshell itself is a separate action and can remove a workspace when no panes remain.

## Open and land

The sidebar’s **Workspaces** selector switches between workspaces and offers **New workspace**. Below it, the sidebar shows the active workspace’s subshells. **Subshells** below the workspace members lets you browse the rest without leaving the active workspace. Opening the app returns you to the workspace you were last in.

1. Open the **⋯** menu beside the workspace selector and choose **Add subshell** to pick an existing session or launch a new one. If no existing subshells are available to add, the new-subshell form opens directly. The new pane joins as a tab. The same action is available beside the grouping dropdown in the **Subshells** section.
2. Selecting a session in **Subshells** adds it to the selected workspace. Included sessions appear above that section; selecting one focuses its tab. With no workspace open, selecting a session opens a workspace that holds it, or creates one. Choose **Subshell dashboard** from the grouping dropdown’s **⋯** menu to open the full dashboard.
3. On a subshell’s own page, **Add a subshell to a workspace** opens a workspace with both subshells as tabs.

## Follow subagents

Active subagents appear beneath their parent subshell. Use the arrow beneath the parent's status dot to expand or collapse them; selecting the already active parent row does the same. They stay inside the parent's terminal and don't create workspace tabs. See [Subagents](/agents/subagents) for harness support and view switching.

## Arrange the panes

In the sidebar, drag a subshell between the workspace area and **Subshells** to add or remove its tab. The centered drop zone says **Add to workspace** or **Remove from workspace**. Removing a tab keeps the session running. The row’s **⋯** menu offers the same actions; removing the last tab retires the workspace, and the session keeps running unfiled.

New subshells join as tabs. On a wide screen, arrange panes side by side: drag a pane's tab toward an edge of another pane and release it when the split preview appears. Each tile group has its own tab row, so tabs sit where you arranged them, not always across the top.

On a small touch screen, the workspace presents its panes as tabs instead of side-by-side terminals. A narrow desktop window with a mouse keeps the draggable pane layout.

## Rename, delete, and the empty case

Open the **⋯** menu beside the workspace picker in the sidebar and choose **Rename…** to change its name; the bar above the panes shows the focused terminal's own name instead. The same menu deletes a workspace outright, which drops all of its panes at once; the sessions keep running, and you can file one into another workspace by selecting it in **Subshells**.

Changing a layout is different from changing a session's working directory or agent settings. Use [Clone and switch presets](/guides/clone) for those launch operations.
