A workspace is the window of panes you work in. Launches land as tabs in one, and a workspace holds references to the running subshells rather than copies of them.

## How they fit together

A subshell is the session; a workspace is how you arrange sessions. The same subshell can sit in several workspaces at once, because a workspace holds a reference, not a copy. Closing a pane in a workspace removes that reference and leaves the session running; closing the subshell itself ends it everywhere (see [Session lifecycle](/guides/lifecycle)).

A workspace exists exactly as long as it holds a tab. Launch your first subshell and you're in a workspace, named after it, with nothing to save. Close the last tab and the workspace retires itself, while its subshells carry on as ungrouped panes.

## Open and land

The sidebar's **Workspaces** tree shows each workspace with its panes, and an **Ungrouped** section for sessions that live in no workspace yet. Opening the app returns you to the workspace you were last in.

1. To start fresh, open the launch dialog from **+ New subshell** in the sidebar. From inside a workspace the pane lands as a new tab; from anywhere else a workspace is opened around it.
2. Selecting a session in the sidebar means its tab: it focuses if it's open on screen, otherwise you go to the workspace that holds it, and a session in no workspace yet gets one. Dragging onto an open workspace files it there; **Add subshell** at the top of the workspace also picks an existing session or launches a new one.
3. On a subshell's own page, the **Split** control puts a second session beside it, which is what creates a workspace out of two related panes.

## Arrange the panes

On a wide screen the dock tiles panes side by side: drag a pane's tab toward an edge of another pane and release it when the split preview appears. Each tile group has its own tab row, so tabs sit where you arranged them, not always across the top.

On a small touch screen, the workspace presents its panes as tabs instead of side-by-side terminals. A narrow desktop window with a mouse keeps the draggable pane layout.

## Rename, delete, and the empty case

Right-click a workspace in the sidebar (or open its **⋯** menu) and choose **Rename…** to change its name; the bar above the panes shows the focused terminal's own name instead. The same menu deletes a workspace outright, which drops all of its panes at once; the sessions keep running ungrouped, and you can file them into another workspace from the Ungrouped section.

Changing a layout is different from changing a session's working directory or agent settings. Use [Clone and switch presets](/guides/clone) for those launch operations.
