A subshell's pane is one process rendering one grid, and it can be open on several screens at once. That fact raises two questions, and this page answers both: who is watching, and why the terminal is the size it is. A shared pane is never watched silently, and the size answer usually involves another device.

## Presence

Every device that attaches to a pane announces itself, and everyone attached sees the full list: their own entry and everyone else's. Each entry carries a **device name**, the grid that device says it can display, when it attached, whether its page is currently rendered, and whether it may type. A [view](/use/sharing) grantee watches and cannot type. When at least two devices are attached, the terminal shows a **Devices** control listing them. While you are the only viewer, there is nothing to list, so the control is absent.

A device name starts life defaulted from the browser, in forms such as "Safari on iPad" and "Firefox on Linux", with a plain fallback when nothing can be derived. The default is deliberately coarse: enough to tell your phone from your laptop, nothing more. You can set your own name. Names are capped at 40 characters and scrubbed of control characters on the way out and again on the way in, because the name you choose is rendered on someone else's screen and in server logs. The Subshell desktop apps announce themselves as such rather than as an ordinary browser page.

> [!note] Push devices are separate
> Enrolling a phone for notifications is a separate, smaller thing: it stores that device's push address under your account, and it lives on the [Notifications](/use/notifications) page.

## One grid, several screens

A tmux pane has exactly one size; there is no per-viewer layout to fall back on. The default rule is **smallest visible viewer wins**: the pane is sized so that every viewer currently rendering it can display all of it, axis by axis. This is the source of the classic "why is my terminal 80 columns on a 4K monitor": a smaller viewer is attached somewhere, and the Devices list names which device holds the width and which holds the height.

The rule has two exceptions, both added because the smallest viewer is sometimes not really watching.

- **Hidden viewers take no part.** A backgrounded browser tab stops laying its page out entirely, so it could not re-fit anyway; it is listed, but it does not hold everyone at phone size. It rejoins the decision the moment it is looked at again. The mobile companion works the same way: it keeps its connection alive in the background for push while reporting itself hidden, so a phone in a pocket never shrinks every laptop watching the same pane.
- **A pinned viewer decides alone.** The Devices control offers *pin the size to this screen*. Pinning is an `edit` act, like typing, because it changes what everyone sees: it names the screen whose size matters. While a pin is held, the smallest-wins rule waits until the pin is released. If the pinned device disconnects, sizing falls back to the default automatically.

A viewer bigger than the pane loses nothing; it just has spare room. A viewer smaller than the pane fits by shrinking its own text rather than clipping rows.

The presence list is shown to everyone, including view-only grantees, because one device silently shrinking everyone else's terminal is otherwise inexplicable. [Sharing](/use/sharing) accounts for the same disclosure from the other side: it is part of what a grant exposes.
